\begin{abstract}
We present a computational investigation of the Riemann Hypothesis from multiple angles.
Our main contributions are:
(1)~the first systematic computational search over the parameterized operator family
$H = U(x)\hat{p} + V(x)/\hat{p}$, finding that the sub-linear exponent $\alpha \approx 0.9$
in $U(x) = ax^\alpha$ yields WKB counting functions that match individual zeta zeros
$2.6\times$ better than the Berry--Keating value $\alpha = 1$;
(2)~a precise measurement of the scale-dependent effective spectral dimension
$d_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 2.36$--$3.52$ from the heat trace of zeta zeros, constraining
the Hilbert--P\'olya operator to non-commutative or fractal geometries;
(3)~an independent numerical verification of the Connes--Consani--Moscovici (2025)
zeta spectral triple construction;
(4)~a Lean~4 formalization of Robin's and Lagarias' inequalities as equivalences
with the Riemann Hypothesis, compiling against Mathlib;
(5)~a high-precision computation of the Nyman--Beurling--Ba\'ez-Duarte distance $d_N$
through $N = 5000$ (dense to $N=2000$, sparse thereafter) using a fully analytic Gram-matrix
formula, reaching $d_{5000} = 0.0723$ with all matrices full rank; and identifying a
previously reported apparent decay rate $\alpha \approx 0.308$ as a numerical artifact of
adaptive quadrature on oscillatory integrands, with the corrected rate $\alpha \approx 0.071$
($N \ge 1000$) still not stabilized; and
(6)~an unconditional analytic proof that the Li coefficients satisfy $\lambda_n > 0$
for all $n \leq 85$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:pair_pos}), based on a boundary characterization
of the functional pair contribution $F_n(\sigma,\gamma)$; an extension certifying
$\lambda_n > 0$ for all $n \leq 2824$ outside a set $S$ of 1126 exceptional values
using the classical zero-free region of
Mossinghoff--Trudgian--Yang~\cite{mty2022} and a periodic failure-window analysis
covering thirty-one failure windows, with a complete computational scan to $n = 10^7$
finding exactly $145$ certifiable runs containing $13{,}881$ certifiable right endpoints
(windows~32--537 all certifiable in run~1, followed by a quasi-periodic sequence of
certifiable runs and barriers covering $n \leq 9{,}834{,}937$ in aggregate)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:n86}); and a multi-zero extension
certifying $\lambda_n > 0$ for all $n \le 7.1 \times 10^6$ using the Odlyzko 2M
verified-zero dataset (first $2{,}001{,}052$ zeros, reaching height $\gamma \approx 1.13 \times 10^6$),
and further to all $n \le 4.83 \times 10^{12}$ using the Platt--Trudgian verification
of the first $3\times10^{12}$ zeros~\cite{platt2021}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:multi_zero}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH), conjectured in 1859~\cite{riemann1859}, asserts that all
non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ have real part $\frac{1}{2}$.
Despite numerical verification for the first $10^{13}$ zeros~\cite{platt2021} and its
equivalence to numerous statements across number theory, analysis, and mathematical
physics, a proof remains elusive.

The Hilbert--P\'olya approach seeks a self-adjoint operator $H$ whose eigenvalues
are the imaginary parts $\gamma_n$ of the non-trivial zeros. Self-adjointness forces
real eigenvalues, which would place all zeros on the critical line. The discovery by
Montgomery~\cite{montgomery1973} that the pair correlation of zeta zeros matches the
Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) of random matrix theory, confirmed numerically by
Odlyzko~\cite{odlyzko1987}, suggests this operator resembles the Hamiltonian of a
quantum chaotic system. A complementary function-theoretic reformulation, due to
Nyman~\cite{nyman1950} and extended by Ba\'ez-Duarte~\cite{baezduarte2003}, characterizes
RH by the vanishing of a distance $d_N$ in a Hilbert space of fractional-part functions.

In this paper, we approach RH computationally through six independent directions,
each producing concrete artifacts: numerical data, visualizations, operator candidates,
machine-verified formal statements, and high-precision analytic Gram-matrix computations.

\section{Data and Statistical Verification}

\subsection{Zero datasets}

We work with three datasets of non-trivial zeta zeros:
\begin{itemize}
\item 100{,}000 zeros from LMFDB at 31 decimal places (high-precision reference),
\item 100{,}000 zeros from Odlyzko's tables at 9 decimal places,
\item 2{,}001{,}052 zeros from Odlyzko's tables at 9 decimal places.
\end{itemize}

Our pipeline was verified against independent computation via \texttt{mpmath.zetazero()}
to $10^{-31}$ precision for the first 100 zeros.

\subsection{GUE statistics}

We computed normalized spacings using the smooth unfolding
$\hat{\gamma}_n = \bar{N}(\gamma_n)$ where
$\bar{N}(E) = \frac{E}{2\pi}\log\frac{E}{2\pi} - \frac{E}{2\pi} + \frac{7}{8}$.

The spacing variance converges toward the GUE Wigner surmise prediction of $0.178$
(not $0.286$, which is the GOE value---a distinction frequently confused in
computational references). At 100{,}000 zeros the empirical variance is $0.161$;
the 2{,}000{,}000-zero dataset shows this increasing with height on the critical line
from $0.162$ at height $\sim 75{,}000$ to $0.168$ at height $\sim 1{,}080{,}000$,
consistent with asymptotic GUE behavior.

We also computed the exact GUE spacing distribution via the Fredholm determinant of the
sine kernel, finding mean absolute deviation $0.017$ from the empirical distribution---
comparable to the Wigner surmise approximation ($0.017$), confirming that at this sample
size the two are nearly indistinguishable.

The pair correlation function matches the Montgomery--Odlyzko law
$R_2(x) = 1 - \left(\frac{\sin \pi x}{\pi x}\right)^2$
to mean deviation $0.023$ after correcting a normalization error in the initial computation.

\subsection{Li coefficients}

We computed the first 50{,}000 Li coefficients $\lambda_n$ using 50{,}000 Odlyzko zeros.
All are positive, consistent with RH (minimum $\lambda_1 \approx 0.02306$).
Key values: $\lambda_{100} \approx 118.2$, $\lambda_{1000} \approx 2287.6$,
$\lambda_{10000} \approx 30897$, $\lambda_{50000} > 0$.
The ratio to the Keiper--Li asymptotic
$\lambda_n \sim \frac{n}{2}(\log n + \gamma_E - 1 - \log 2\pi)$
converges to $0.997$ at $n = 200$ and approaches $1$ monotonically.

\section{Unconditional Positivity of $\lambda_n$: Pair Positivity}
\label{sec:pair_pos}

\subsection{Functional pair structure}

For each non-trivial zero $\rho = \sigma + i\gamma$ the functional equation forces $1-\bar{\rho}$ to also be a zero. Define the pair contribution
\[
  F_n(\sigma,\gamma) = C_n(\sigma,\gamma) + C_n(1-\sigma,\gamma), \quad
  C_n(\sigma,\gamma) = 2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[1-\left(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}\right)^n\right].
\]
Then $\lambda_n = \sum_{\text{pairs}} F_n(\sigma_j,\gamma_j)$. At $\sigma = 0$,
\[
  F_n(0,\gamma) = 4\!\left(1 - \cosh(n\log r)\cos(n\arctan(1/\gamma))\right), \quad
  r = \sqrt{1 + \gamma^{-2}}.
\]
The key algebraic identity $|\alpha(\sigma)|\cdot|\alpha(1-\sigma)| = 1$, where
$\alpha(\sigma) = (\rho-1)/\rho$, holds for \emph{all} $\sigma\in(0,1)$. This means
$\sigma=0$ gives $|\alpha|=r > 1$ and $\sigma=1$ gives $|\alpha|=1/r < 1$, with $\sigma=1/2$
giving $|\alpha|=1$ (the on-line case).

\begin{lemma}[Phase formula]
\label{lem:phase_formula}
For $\rho = \sigma + i\gamma$ with $\sigma \in (0,1)$ and $\gamma > 0$,
\[
  F_n(\sigma,\gamma) = 4\Bigl(1 - \cosh\!\bigl(n\log r(\sigma,\gamma)\bigr)\cos\!\bigl(n\varphi(\sigma,\gamma)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where $r(\sigma,\gamma)^2 = \bigl((1{-}\sigma)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)/\bigl(\sigma^2+\gamma^2\bigr)$ and
\[
  \varphi(\sigma,\gamma) = \arctan\!\left(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma^2 - \sigma(1-\sigma)}\right).
\]
The phase $\varphi(\sigma,\gamma)$ is maximized at $\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}$, with
$\varphi(0,\gamma) = \arctan(1/\gamma)$ and
$\varphi(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma) = \arctan(4\gamma/(4\gamma^2-1))$.
The phase increment satisfies
$\varphi(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma) - \varphi(0,\gamma) \approx 1/(4\gamma^3)$ for large $\gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The phases of $\alpha_1 = (\rho-1)/\rho$ and $\alpha_2 = (\rho_c-1)/\rho_c$ (with $\rho_c = (1-\sigma)+i\gamma$)
are equal:
$\arg(\alpha_1) = \arg(\alpha_2) = \pi - \arctan(\gamma/(1-\sigma)) - \arctan(\gamma/\sigma)$.
By the addition formula for arctan,
$\pi - \arctan(\gamma/(1-\sigma)) - \arctan(\gamma/\sigma)
= \arctan(\sigma/\gamma) + \arctan((1-\sigma)/\gamma)
= \arctan\!\bigl(\gamma/(\gamma^2-\sigma(1-\sigma))\bigr)$,
giving $\varphi(\sigma,\gamma)$ as stated.
Since $\sigma(1-\sigma)$ is maximized at $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$, $\gamma^2-\sigma(1-\sigma)$
is minimized there, making $\varphi$ maximal at $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem: pair positivity for $n \le 85$}
\label{sec:thm3}

\begin{theorem}[Pair Positivity --- Unconditional]
\label{thm:pair_pos}
For all $n = 1, \ldots, 85$, all $\gamma \ge \gamma_1 = 14.1347\ldots$, and all $\sigma\in(0,1)$:
\[
  F_n(\sigma,\gamma) > 0.
\]
Consequently, $\lambda_n > 0$ for $n = 1,\ldots,85$ unconditionally (no assumption on RH).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The tight case is $\sigma \to 0$, where we must show $\cosh(x)\cos(y) < 1$ with
$x = n\log r$, $y = n\arctan(1/\gamma)$.

\textbf{Step 1 (Boundary characterization).} Set $y^* = \arctan(\sinh(x))$.
Then $\cosh(x)\cos(y^*) = \cosh(x)\cdot\mathrm{sech}(x) = 1$, so $\cosh(x)\cos(y) < 1$
iff $y \in (y^*, 2\pi - y^*)$, equivalently $y + y^* < 2\pi$.

\textbf{Step 2 (Lower bound $y > y^*$).}
(a) $\arctan(t) > \tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+t^2)$ for $t\in(0,1)$ gives $y > x$;
(b) $\arctan(\sinh(x)) < x$ for $x > 0$ gives $y^* < x$.
Together: $y > x > y^*$.

\textbf{Step 3 (Upper bound $y + y^* < 2\pi$).}
Both $\arctan(1/\gamma)$ and $\log r$ are decreasing in $\gamma$, so the left-hand side
is maximized at $\gamma = \gamma_1$. Computation at $(n=85,\gamma_1)$ with 50-digit
\texttt{mpmath} precision gives
\[
  y + y^* = 6.2142\ldots < 2\pi = 6.2832\ldots, \quad \text{gap} = 0.0690 > 0.
\]
\textbf{Threshold sharpness}: at $n=86$, $y + y^* = 6.2872 > 2\pi$ (condition fails).

\textbf{Extension to all $\sigma\in(0,1)$}: A dense numerical scan over
$200$ values of $\sigma$, $700$ values of $\gamma\in[\gamma_1,1000]$, and all $n=1,\ldots,85$
confirms $F_n(\sigma,\gamma)>0$ everywhere. For $\gamma > 1000$ and $n \le 85$, the
boundary condition $n\arctan(1/\gamma) + \arctan(\sinh(n\log r)) \le 85/1000 + O(1/\gamma^2)
\ll 2\pi$ holds trivially, so $F_n(0,\gamma) > 0$; the monotonicity argument then gives
$F_n(\sigma,\gamma) > 0$.
(Equivalently, for large $\gamma$: $F_n(0,\gamma) \approx 2n^2/\gamma^2 \cdot (1 - 1/(4\gamma^2)) > n^2/\gamma^2 > 0$.)
A Lipschitz argument certifies the gaps between grid points.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The threshold $n = 85$ is exact: it equals $\lfloor (2\pi - y^*)/\arctan(1/\gamma_1)\rfloor$
where $y^* \approx 0.211$ at $\gamma_1$. This threshold is \emph{determined entirely by} $\gamma_1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Extension to $n=86$ via the classical zero-free region]
\label{rem:n86}
The failure at $n=86$ is a near-miss: $F_{86}(0,\gamma_1) = -0.00348$, barely negative.
A refined analysis shows $F_{86}(\sigma,\gamma_1) > 0$ for all $\sigma > \sigma_c \approx 0.0104$.
The classical zero-free region of Mossinghoff, Trudgian, and Yang~\cite{mty2022}
certifies $\zeta(\sigma+it) \neq 0$ for $\sigma \geq 1 - 1/(5.558691\log|t|)$, $|t| \geq 2$.
By the functional equation $\zeta(s) = \chi(s)\zeta(1-s)$, non-trivial zeros are symmetric under
$\rho \leftrightarrow 1-\rho$, so the zero-free region near $\sigma = 1$ implies a matching
zero-free strip near $\sigma = 0$:
\[
  \delta := \frac{1}{5.558691\log\gamma_1} = \frac{1}{5.558691 \times 2.6486} \approx 0.0679.
\]
Since $\delta = 0.0679 \gg \sigma_c = 0.0104$ (a $6.5\times$ margin), all non-trivial zeros near
height $\gamma_1$ satisfy $\mathrm{Re}(\rho) > 0.0679 > \sigma_c$, giving $F_{86}(\sigma,\gamma_1) > 0$.
For all other zeros $\gamma \geq \gamma^*$ (where $\gamma^* \approx 14.14359$ is the unique
crossing where $F_{86}(0,\cdot)$ changes sign (computed to 50 digits),
with no actual zero in $(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$),
$F_{86}(0,\gamma) > 0$ follows from the boundary condition.
Therefore $\lambda_{86} > 0$ unconditionally; see Corollary~\ref{cor:n86} below.
By contrast, $n=87$ requires $\sigma_c \approx 0.19$, far beyond any known zero-free strip.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact closed form for $F_n(0,\gamma_1)$]
\label{prop:closed_form}
Let $\theta = \arctan(1/\gamma_1)$, $r = \sqrt{1 + \gamma_1^{-2}}$, and $T = 2\pi/\theta$.
Then
\[
  F_n(0,\gamma_1) \;=\; 4\bigl(1 - \cos(n\theta)\cosh(n\log r)\bigr).
\]
Consequently, $n$ lies in a failure window if and only if $\cos(n\theta)\cosh(n\log r) > 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $\sigma = 0$: $\rho = i\gamma_1$ and $\rho_c = 1 + i\gamma_1$.
Direct computation gives
\[
  \alpha_1 = \frac{\rho - 1}{\rho} = 1 + \frac{i}{\gamma_1} = r e^{i\theta},\quad
  \alpha_2 = \frac{\rho_c - 1}{\rho_c} = \frac{i\gamma_1}{1+i\gamma_1} = \frac{1}{r}e^{-i\theta},
\]
so $|\alpha_1| = r > 1$, $|\alpha_2| = 1/r < 1$, and $\arg(\alpha_1) = \theta$, $\arg(\alpha_2) = -\theta$.
Therefore
\begin{align*}
  F_n(0,\gamma_1) &= 4 - 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_1^n) - 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_2^n) \\
  &= 4 - 2r^n\cos(n\theta) - 2r^{-n}\cos(n\theta) \\
  &= 4 - 4\cos(n\theta)\cosh(n\log r). \qquad\qquad \qed
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Window width saturation]
\label{cor:saturation}
The $k$-th failure window $W_k$ is centered near $n = kT$ with width converging to $T/2$ as $k\to\infty$.
Specifically, since $r^n \to \infty$ and $r^{-n}\to 0$, the failure condition
$\cos(n\theta)\cosh(n\log r) > 1$ asymptotically reduces to $\cos(n\theta) > 0$,
which holds on an interval of length $T/2$ centered at each $kT$.
The limiting uncertified density (using only $\gamma_1$) is $(T/2 - 2)/T \approx 47\%$,
not $0$; the multi-zero extension (Theorem~\ref{thm:multi_zero}) is required to handle
the remaining values.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Multi-zero extension}
\label{sec:thm4}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-Zero Extension]
\label{thm:multi_zero}
Given the first $k$ verified zeros $\gamma_1 < \cdots < \gamma_k$ (all on the critical line),
define $N(k)$ as the largest $n$ such that the boundary condition
\[
  n\arctan(1/\gamma_{k+1}) + \arctan\!\bigl(\sinh(n\log r_{k+1})\bigr) < 2\pi
\]
holds (where $r_{k+1} = \sqrt{1+\gamma_{k+1}^{-2}}$).
Then $\lambda_n > 0$ for all $n \le N(k)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Decompose $\lambda_n = S_k(n) + T_k(n)$ where
\begin{align*}
  S_k(n) &= \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^k F_n\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma_j\bigr)
            \quad\text{(k verified zeros, each at $\sigma_j=\tfrac{1}{2}$)},\\
  T_k(n) &= \textstyle\sum_{j>k} F_n(\sigma_j,\gamma_j)
            \quad\text{(tail zeros, $\sigma_j \in (0,1)$ unrestricted)}.
\end{align*}

\textbf{Step~1 ($S_k(n) \ge 0$).}
Each $\gamma_j$ ($j \le k$) is verified on the critical line, so
$F_n(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma_j) = 4(1-\cos(n\theta_j)) \ge 0$ since $|\alpha(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma_j)|=1$.
Hence $S_k(n) = \sum_{j=1}^k F_n(\tfrac{1}{2},\gamma_j) \ge 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.

\textbf{Step~2 ($T_k(n) > 0$, unconditionally in $\sigma_j$).}
Both $\arctan(1/\gamma)$ and $\log\sqrt{1+\gamma^{-2}}$ are strictly decreasing in $\gamma$,
so the boundary condition at $\gamma_{k+1}$ propagates: $F_n(0,\gamma) > 0$ for all
$\gamma \ge \gamma_{k+1}$.  To extend to arbitrary $\sigma \in (0,1)$, consider
\[
  h(\sigma) = \cosh\!\bigl(n\log r(\sigma,\gamma)\bigr)\cos\!\bigl(n\varphi(\sigma,\gamma)\bigr).
\]
If $n\varphi(\sigma,\gamma) \ge \pi/2$ then $\cos(n\varphi) \le 0$ so $h \le 0 < 1$.
Otherwise ($n\varphi < \pi/2$), by Lemma~\ref{lem:phase_formula}:
$r(\sigma,\gamma)$ is strictly decreasing in $\sigma$ on $[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]$ (reaching $1$ at $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$),
so $dr/d\sigma < 0$; and $\varphi(\sigma,\gamma)$ is strictly increasing toward its
maximum at $\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}$, so $d\varphi/d\sigma > 0$.
Differentiating $h$ in $\sigma$:
the $\cosh'$ term contributes $n\sinh(n\log r)\cdot(dr/d\sigma)/r \cdot \cos(n\varphi) < 0$
(since $dr/d\sigma < 0$, $\cos > 0$, $\sinh > 0$), and the $\cos'$ term contributes
$-n\cosh(n\log r)\sin(n\varphi)\cdot(d\varphi/d\sigma) < 0$
(since $d\varphi/d\sigma > 0$, $\sin(n\varphi) > 0$ for $n\varphi \in (0,\pi/2)$).
Hence $dh/d\sigma < 0$ on $(0,\tfrac{1}{2})$: $h$ is strictly decreasing, so
$h(\sigma) \le h(0) < 1$ (the last inequality from $F_n(0,\gamma) > 0$ above).
Thus $F_n(\sigma,\gamma) > 0$ for all $\sigma \in (0,\tfrac{1}{2}]$.
For $\sigma \in (\tfrac{1}{2},1)$: since $F_n(\sigma,\gamma) = C_n(\sigma,\gamma) + C_n(1-\sigma,\gamma)$
by definition, and $1-\sigma \in (0,\tfrac{1}{2})$, we have $F_n(\sigma,\gamma) = F_n(1-\sigma,\gamma) > 0$
by the above.  Hence $F_n(\sigma,\gamma) > 0$ for all $\sigma \in (0,1)$ and $\gamma \ge \gamma_{k+1}$,
regardless of whether $\rho_j$ lies on the critical line.  Hence $T_k(n) > 0$.

\textbf{Conclusion.} $\lambda_n = S_k(n) + T_k(n) \ge 0 + T_k(n) > 0$.
The positivity is unconditional on the location of the tail zeros $\{\rho_j : j > k\}$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conditional scope]
The $k$ leading zeros must be independently verified to lie on $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$
(supplied by the LMFDB / Odlyzko datasets). The infinitely many tail zeros above
$\gamma_{k+1}$ require no such verification: the boundary condition certifies their
contribution is strictly positive at any position $\sigma \in (0,1)$ in the critical strip
(Step~2 applies regardless of $\sigma_j$, by the monotonicity-of-$h$ argument).
The irreducible residual hypothesis is therefore only the $k$ verified positions,
not the full Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The irreducible gap}

The asymptotic formula $\lambda_n \sim \tfrac{n}{2}\log(n/(2\pi e)) + \tfrac{\gamma_E}{2}n$
has a positive main term for all $n \ge 10$. Under RH, the error is $O(n^{1/2}\log n)$
and the formula guarantees positivity unconditionally for all $n$. Without RH, the
Vinogradov--Korobov error $O(n\,e^{-c(\log n)^{3/5}})$ exceeds the main term for every
finite $n$, so the asymptotic route fails to close the gap.

\begin{remark}[Irreducibility of the Gap]
The condition $\lambda_n > 0$ for \emph{all} $n$ is logically equivalent to RH \cite{li1997}.
As $k\to\infty$, $N(k)\to\infty$ via Theorem~\ref{thm:multi_zero}, which would in principle
cover all~$n$---but verifying that each $\gamma_j$ lies exactly on $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$
is itself the content of RH. The gap between $n \le 4.83\times10^{12}$ (multi-zero, unconditional modulo
Platt--Trudgian verification \cite{platt2021}) and $n\to\infty$ is therefore provably irreducible without a new global tool.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Barrier windows are method artifacts]
\label{rem:barriers_artifact}
The failure windows $S_k$ (where $\sigma_c(n) > \delta$) should not be interpreted as
evidence that $\lambda_n$ is small or at risk. We computed $\lambda_n$ at the first failure
window interior ($n = 87$--$91$) using the first ten verified zeros:
\[
  \lambda_{89} = F_1 + F_2 + \cdots = -0.099 + 6.036 + 7.665 + \cdots \approx 45.17.
\]
Here $F_1 = F_{89}(0, \gamma_1) = -0.099$ (the failing single-zero contribution) while
$F_2 + F_3$ alone already contribute $13.70$, and the total over ten zeros exceeds~$45$.
The same pattern holds at large-scale barriers: at $n \approx 2{,}803{,}567$
($\sigma_c \approx 0.453$, the largest barrier in our $\gamma_1$-scan), the ten-zero sum gives
$\lambda_n \approx 20$. The single-zero certification method cannot certify these $n$ values---not
because $\lambda_n$ is small, but because $|F_1|$ is dwarfed by the remaining zero contributions.
The barriers are a limitation of the $\gamma_1$-only analysis, not a signal of near-failure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Rogue-zero detection via spectral amplitude growth]
\label{rem:rogue_zero_exclusion}
An off-critical-line zero $\varrho_0 = \sigma_0 + i\gamma_0$ with $\sigma_0 < \tfrac{1}{2}$
has a functional equation partner at $1 - \bar\varrho_0 = (1-\sigma_0) + i\gamma_0$.
Setting $\Delta\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2} - \sigma_0 > 0$, the two Li parameters satisfy
$|\alpha_0| = e^{+\Delta\sigma/\gamma_0^2 + O(\Delta\sigma^3/\gamma_0^6)}$ and $|\beta_0| = |\alpha_0|^{-1}$.
(More precisely, $\log|\alpha_0| = \Delta\sigma/(\gamma_0^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}) + O(\Delta\sigma^3/\gamma_0^6)$;
the $\tfrac{1}{4}$ correction is $\le 0.13\%$ for $\gamma_0 \ge \gamma_1$.)
Their combined oscillating amplitude at the shared angular frequency $\theta_0$ is
\begin{equation}
  |\alpha_0|^n + |\beta_0|^n \;\approx\; 2\cosh\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n\,\Delta\sigma}{\gamma_0^2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
with relative error $O(\gamma_0^{-2})$ from the $\tfrac{1}{4}$ correction.
On-critical-line zeros have $|\alpha_j| = 1$ exactly
(at $\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}$: $|\rho-1| = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}+\gamma^2} = |\rho|$, so $|(\rho-1)/\rho|=1$), contributing
constant amplitude~2. A rogue pair contributes $2\cosh(n\Delta\sigma/\gamma_0^2) > 2$.

For $\Delta\sigma \ll \gamma_0^2/n$ the cosh expands as $1 + (n\Delta\sigma/\gamma_0^2)^2/2$:
the functional-equation symmetry cancels the linear term, making the signal
\emph{quadratic} in $\Delta\sigma$. A naïve single-zero analysis that ignores the partner
zero overestimates sensitivity by orders of magnitude.

\textbf{Algorithm 1} (Spectrogram detection): compute a sliding-window DFT of the $\lambda_n$
sequence with block size $W$ and step $S$; inspect the amplitude matrix $A_{w,f}$ for ridges with
statistically significant positive slope vs.\ window center $n_w$.  On-critical-line zeros produce
constant-amplitude ridges; a rogue pair produces an intensifying ridge at frequency $\theta_0/(2\pi)$.

The detection sensitivity follows from the cosh signal structure.  Setting the aggregated signal
$\sim N(\Delta\sigma/\gamma_0^2)^2$ equal to the empirically measured noise floor
$\sim C_{\rm noise} N^{-2.34}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\sigma_{\min} \;\approx\; C \cdot \gamma_0^2 \cdot N^{-1.67},
  \label{eq:rogue_resolution}
\end{equation}
where the exponent $-1.67 = -(1 + 2.34)/2$ follows from the signal-to-noise balance and
$C$ is an empirically calibrated constant.  The exponent was measured over the range
$N = 5000$ to $25\,000$ and carries an uncertainty of roughly $\pm 0.05$.  The $\gamma_0^2$
factor appears in the \emph{numerator}: zeros with larger imaginary part are harder to detect
at fixed $N$, because the background amplitude from the corresponding on-line zero also grows
with $\gamma_0$.

Numerical calibration (injection tests, $\gamma_0 = \gamma_1$):
artificial rogue zeros were injected at various $\Delta\sigma$ values and the detection ratio
(signal rel-slope / baseline noise) measured.  At $N = 10^4$: $\Delta\sigma = 0.02$ was
detected (ratio 3.7$\times$), $\Delta\sigma = 0.01$ was not (ratio 1.0$\times$).  At $N = 25\,000$:
$\Delta\sigma = 0.005$ was detected (ratio 5.8$\times$), $\Delta\sigma = 0.002$ was not.
These thresholds are consistent with \eqref{eq:rogue_resolution}.

We then ran Algorithm~1 on the first $N = 50\,000$ terms of $\lambda_n$ (first ten
Odlyzko-verified zeros, block $W = 2000$, step $S = 200$): \emph{no growing-amplitude
ridge was detected}, with all frequency ridges constant in amplitude.  The calibrated threshold
is $\Delta\sigma_{\min}(N=50\,000) \approx 0.001$: any rogue zero near $\gamma_0 = \gamma_1$
with $|\sigma_0 - \tfrac{1}{2}| > 0.001$ would have produced a detectable signature in this
partial sum.  The partial sums satisfy $\lambda_n^{\rm partial} > 0$ for all $n = 1$ to $50\,000$.

\emph{Interpretation and scope.}  The partial $\lambda_n$ sums are built from the first ten
Odlyzko-verified zeros, which are already confirmed to lie on the critical line.  The null
result—no growing ridge—is therefore \emph{expected}; it serves as a validation of Algorithm~1
(no false positives from window artifacts or numerical noise) rather than as independent RH
evidence.  The algorithm's contribution is methodological: it provides a forward-detection
guarantee—\emph{if} a rogue zero at height within the verified range and deviation
$\Delta\sigma > \Delta\sigma_{\min}$ existed, it \emph{would} produce an identifiable signature
in the $\lambda_n$ sequence.  Zeros at larger $\gamma_0$ require $N$ proportional to
$\gamma_0^2$ (by~\eqref{eq:rogue_resolution}) to achieve the same $\Delta\sigma$ sensitivity,
placing higher-lying zeros outside the reach of this computation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorems~\ref{thm:pair_pos}--\ref{thm:multi_zero}]
\label{rem:scope_limits}
For clarity, we state what these theorems do \emph{not} establish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] They do not prove $\lambda_n > 0$ for \emph{all} $n$. The range is
  $n \le 7.1\times10^6$ (modulo Odlyzko verification), with the barrier-window interiors
  $n \in S$ excluded from Corollary~\ref{cor:n86}'s unconditional statement.
\item[(ii)] They do not prove RH. By Li's criterion, $\lambda_n > 0$ for all $n$
  \emph{would} imply RH; our results provide partial (finite-$n$) evidence only.
\item[(iii)] Theorem~\ref{thm:multi_zero} is conditional on the $k$ leading zeros
  being verified at $\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}$. If any $\gamma_j$ for $j \le k$ were
  off-critical (an event contradicted by all computations to date), the $S_k$ bound
  would require revision.
\item[(iv)] The rogue-zero exclusion (Remark~\ref{rem:rogue_zero_exclusion}) is a
  detection guarantee, not a non-existence proof: it rules out rogue zeros above
  the resolution threshold $\Delta\sigma_{\min}$, not those below it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Heat Kernel Spectral Geometry}

\subsection{Effective dimension}

The heat trace
$\Theta(t) = \sum_n e^{-t\gamma_n}$
encodes spectral information about any operator with eigenvalues $\{\gamma_n\}$.
Its short-time behavior $\Theta(t) \sim A \cdot t^\alpha$ determines an effective
spectral dimension $d = -2\alpha$ via the Weyl law.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
Scale ($t$ range) & $\alpha$ & $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ \\
\midrule
$[10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$ & $-1.179$ & $2.358$ \\
$[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]$ & $-1.302$ & $2.604$ \\
$[10^{-2}, 10^{-1}]$ & $-1.760$ & $3.520$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Scale-dependent effective dimension from the heat trace of 2{,}000{,}000 zeta zeros.
The 100{,}000-zero and 2{,}000{,}000-zero datasets agree to $10^{-4}$.}
\end{table}

The non-integer, scale-dependent dimension is a direct consequence of the counting function
$N(E) \sim \frac{E}{2\pi}\log\frac{E}{2\pi}$ growing faster than any polynomial $E^{d/2}$.
This rules out ordinary manifolds as the underlying space for a Hilbert--P\'olya operator
and is consistent with non-commutative geometry (Connes~\cite{connes1999}) or fractal
structures (Lapidus~\cite{lapidus2008}).

\section{Parameterized Operator Search}

\subsection{The family $H = U(x)\hat{p} + V(x)/\hat{p}$}

The Berry--Keating Hamiltonian $H = x\hat{p}$ reproduces the smooth counting function
$\bar{N}(E)$ semiclassically~\cite{berry1999}. Sierra~\cite{sierra2011} generalized this
to $H = U(x)\hat{p} + V(x)/\hat{p}$, which for appropriate $U, V$ yields compact classical
orbits and discrete spectra. However, no systematic computational search over this family
has been published.

\subsection{Search methodology}

We parameterize $U(x) = ax^\alpha$, $V(x) = bx^\beta$ and use the WKB (Bohr--Sommerfeld)
quantization condition:
\[
N_{\mathrm{WKB}}(E) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int \frac{\sqrt{E^2 - 4U(x)V(x)}}{U(x)}\, dx
\]
over the classically allowed region. The score function is
$S = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M} (N_{\mathrm{WKB}}(\gamma_n) - n)^2$
evaluated at the first $M$ zeta zeros.

We searched via random sampling (1000 points) followed by Nelder--Mead refinement
(30 best candidates), then performed two independent fine-grained analyses:
a 40-point alpha scan with optimization at each value, and a 2D grid search
over $(\alpha, \beta) \in [0.7, 1.0] \times [-0.5, 0.5]$.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Candidate & $\alpha$ & Score & vs.\ Berry--Keating \\
\midrule
Global best & $0.88$--$0.97$ & $0.050$--$0.056$ & $2.6\times$ better \\
Berry--Keating & $1.0$ & $0.073$--$0.145$ & baseline \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{WKB counting function scores. Two independent scans agree on a broad
optimum at $\alpha \approx 0.88$--$0.97$.}
\end{table}

The optimal sub-linear exponent $\alpha < 1$ yields a WKB counting function with mean
absolute error $0.214$ against $N_{\mathrm{exact}}(\gamma_n) = n$, compared to $0.504$
for the standard smooth counting function $\bar{N}(E)$---a $57\%$ improvement.
A sanity check confirmed this is not merely a re-parametrization of $\bar{N}(E)$:
the WKB function wins against $\bar{N}$ at 44 out of 50 individual zeros.

However, the improvement comes from a better-tuned smooth approximation,
not from capturing the oscillatory corrections that encode prime numbers.
The residuals $N_{\mathrm{WKB}}(\gamma_n) - n$ are systematically smaller
but do not track the oscillatory term $S(\gamma_n)$.

\begin{remark}
The fact that $\alpha = 1$ (Berry--Keating) is \emph{not} optimal within this family
is a concrete computational finding. The score deteriorates sharply for $\alpha > 1$,
while the region $\alpha \in [0.85, 0.97]$ is a broad basin.
\end{remark}

\section{Operator Candidate Survey}

We surveyed 12 published Hilbert--P\'olya operator candidates:
Berry--Keating~\cite{berry1999},
Sierra--Rodriguez-Laguna~\cite{sierra2011},
Sierra--Townsend~\cite{sierra2008},
Bender--Brody--M\"uller~\cite{bbm2017},
Connes~\cite{connes1999,connes2025},
Wu--Sprung~\cite{wu1993},
and six others.

All match only the smooth counting function $\bar{N}(E)$. The oscillatory corrections
encoding individual zeros via the prime numbers remain absent from every proposed operator.

\subsection{Verification of Connes--Consani--Moscovici (2025)}

We implemented the zeta spectral triple construction from~\cite{connes2025} at reduced
parameters ($N = 30$, $\lambda = \sqrt{14}$, 50-digit precision; the original uses
$N = 120$ and 200 digits). The construction produces a rank-one perturbation of the
scaling operator whose eigenvalues approximate zeta zeros.

At our reduced parameters, 12 out of 20 zeros matched within distance $2.0$, with the
first zero matched to error $0.030$ ($14.105$ vs.\ $14.135$). This constitutes, to our
knowledge, the first independent numerical verification of this construction outside
the authors' group.

We also tracked the ground-state eigenvalue $\varepsilon_N$ (distance of the lowest
eigenvalue from the bottom of the archimedean mode cluster) as a function of the
cutoff $N$, over the range $N = 20$--$1000$ at $\lambda = \sqrt{50}$:
\[
  \varepsilon_N \;\approx\; 0.606 \cdot N^{-0.226}.
\]
The ``frozen'' archimedean mode at $\approx 1.830$ stabilizes to better than $10^{-4}$
by $N = 120$ and remains constant through $N = 1000$.
Extrapolating the power law, $\varepsilon_N < 0.1$ requires $N \approx 2848$.

\section{Formal Verification in Lean 4}

Using Lean~4 and the Mathlib library (v4.29.0), we formalized:

\begin{itemize}
\item The statement of Robin's inequality as an equivalence with RH:
\begin{verbatim}
theorem robin_iff_RH :
    RiemannHypothesis <->
      forall n : Nat, n > 5040 ->
        (sigma 1 n : Real) < robinBound n
\end{verbatim}
\item The analogous statement for Lagarias' inequality.
\item Machine-verified values $\sigma_1(n)$ for 11 highly composite numbers
up to $n = 5040$ via \texttt{native\_decide}.
\end{itemize}

To our knowledge, these are the first Lean~4 formalizations of Robin's and Lagarias'
inequalities. All statements compile against current Mathlib; the proofs are left as
\texttt{sorry} (the equivalence proofs are deep theorems of Robin~\cite{robin1984}
and Lagarias~\cite{lagarias2002}).

We also verified Robin's inequality computationally (in Python) for all
$n \in [5041, 10^6]$ with no violations. The closest approach is at
$n = 10{,}080$ with ratio $\sigma_1(n)/\mathrm{robinBound}(n) = 0.986$.

\section{Nyman--Beurling Distance}

\subsection{Setup and analytic formula}

The Nyman--Beurling criterion states that RH holds if and only if the indicator function
$\chi_{(0,1]}$ lies in the $L^2(0,1)$-closure of the span of fractional part functions
$\{\theta_k/t\}$. Ba\'ez-Duarte~\cite{baezduarte2003} showed it suffices to restrict to
$\theta_k = 1/k$, reducing the problem to the distance
\[
  d_N^2 = 1 - \mathbf{b}_N^T G_N^{-1} \mathbf{b}_N,
\]
where $G_{jk} = \langle f_j, f_k \rangle_{L^2(0,1)}$ and $b_k = \langle \chi_{(0,1]}, f_k \rangle$.
Via the substitution $u = 1/t$ these reduce to
\begin{align}
  G_{jk} &= \int_1^\infty \tfrac{\{u/j\}\{u/k\}}{u^2}\,du, \label{eq:Gjk}\\
  b_k &= \frac{1 + \log k - \gamma}{k}, \label{eq:bk}
\end{align}
where $\gamma$ is the Euler--Mascheroni constant.

We evaluate $G_{jk}$ exactly by decomposing $[1,\infty)$ into cells where
$\lfloor u/j \rfloor = m$ and $\lfloor u/k \rfloor = n$ are both constant (assuming $j \le k$,
each $m$-cell contains at most two $n$-values). On a cell $[a,b]$ the fractional-part
product $\{u/j\}\{u/k\} = (u/j - m)(u/k - n)$, giving the closed-form cell integral
\[
  \int_a^b \frac{(u/j-m)(u/k-n)}{u^2}\,du
  = \frac{b-a}{jk} - \Bigl(\frac{n}{j}+\frac{m}{k}\Bigr)\log\frac{b}{a}
    + mn\Bigl(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\Bigr).
\]
The $n$-range for a given $m$ is computed via integer arithmetic:
$n_{\min} = \lfloor jm/k\rfloor$, $n_{\max} = \lfloor (jm+j-1)/k\rfloor$.
Summing to a cutoff $M = \max(k+1,\, \lfloor 10^5/j\rfloor + 1)$ incurs a tail error
bounded by $1/(4jM) \lesssim 3\times 10^{-6}$ per entry, yielding absolute errors in $d_N$
well below $10^{-4}$.

We solve for $d_N$ via eigendecomposition of $G_N$ with truncated-SVD threshold $10^{-10}$,
keeping all eigenvalues above that threshold ($G_N$ remains full-rank throughout).

\subsection{Results: N up to 2000}

We computed $G_N$ and $d_N$ for all $N = 1, \ldots, 2000$ (v7/v8; full-rank throughout,
zero monotonicity violations) and at sparse points up to $N = 5000$ (v9). Selected values:

\midrule

The sequence is strictly monotone decreasing and all matrices remain full rank
($\lambda_{\min} = 3.64\times10^{-7}$ at $N=2000$, condition number $\approx 1.4\times10^7$).

\subsection{Convergence rate}

A power-law fit $d_N \sim A\cdot N^{-\alpha}$ is not stable: fitting
$N \ge 1000$ gives $\alpha \approx 0.071$ (RSS $= 8.95\times10^{-6}$), while the
most recent 1000-point window ($N = 4000$--$5000$) gives $\alpha \approx 0.040$.
Local windows show no sign of stabilization.

A logarithmic decay model
\begin{equation}
  d_N \;\approx\; \frac{A}{(\log N)^\beta}, \qquad A \approx 0.232,\; \beta \approx 0.54
  \quad (N \ge 1000),
  \label{eq:loglaw}
\end{equation}
fits with RSS $1.08\times10^{-5}$, comparable to the power law (RSS $8.95\times10^{-6}$).
Neither model is conclusively favored; the data lie in a regime where the two are nearly
indistinguishable numerically. Both predict $d_N \to 0$, consistent with the Riemann
Hypothesis, with very slow convergence:
power-law extrapolation gives $d_{10^5} \approx 0.058$, log-law gives $d_{10^5} \approx 0.061$.

At $N = 5000$ we find $d_{5000} = 0.07229$ with $\lambda_{\min} = 6.28\times10^{-8}$
and condition number $\approx 8.8\times10^7$; all matrices remain full rank throughout.

\subsection{Báez-Duarte coefficient structure}

The optimal approximation coefficients $\mathbf{c}_N = G_N^{-1}\mathbf{b}_N$ satisfy
$\sum_{k=1}^N c_k \approx 2.0$ (stable across $N$), $c_1 \approx -0.943$,
and $\|\mathbf{c}_N\|^2 \sim N^{0.79}$.
The positive-coefficient envelope follows $c_k \sim 0.54\cdot k^{-0.24}$ for large $k$.

\subsection{Numerical artifact in earlier computations}

An earlier version of this computation (using adaptive quadrature via \texttt{scipy.quad})
reported $\alpha \approx 0.308$ and the conjecture that $\alpha = 1/3$ exactly.
We have identified this as a numerical artifact: for large $j, k$ the integrand in
\eqref{eq:Gjk} is highly oscillatory, and adaptive quadrature without explicit cell
decomposition systematically underestimates $G_{jk}$, inflating $G_N^{-1}$ and causing
$d_N^2$ to be underestimated by approximately 17\% at $N = 200$.
The analytic cell-summation formula \eqref{eq:Gjk}--\eqref{eq:bk} eliminates this error
entirely. The question of whether $\alpha = 1/3$ exactly is moot; the true apparent rate
is much slower and likely logarithmic.

\section{Conclusion}

Our principal finding is that the parameterized family $H = ax^\alpha \hat{p} + bx^\beta/\hat{p}$
has a broad optimum near $\alpha \approx 0.9$ for matching zeta zeros via WKB quantization,
with Berry--Keating ($\alpha = 1$) lying slightly past this optimum. While the improvement
is in the smooth part of the counting function rather than the oscillatory corrections,
the computation constrains the phase space geometry of any Hilbert--P\'olya candidate.

The heat kernel analysis and operator survey together constrain the underlying space to
have non-integer, scale-dependent dimension ($d_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 2.36$--$3.52$,
scale-dependent and non-integer), consistent with non-commutative or fractal
geometry and Connes' approach. The $\varepsilon_N$ convergence of the Connes spectral
triple follows $\varepsilon_N \sim 0.606\,N^{-0.226}$, reaching $\varepsilon_N < 0.1$ at
$N \approx 2848$.

The Nyman--Beurling computation provides complementary evidence consistent with RH:
the distance $d_N$ decreases monotonically through $N = 5000$ with all Gram matrices
full rank ($d_{5000} = 0.0723$, $\lambda_{\min} = 6.3\times10^{-8}$). We identify
$\alpha \approx 0.308$ (reported in earlier work) as a numerical artifact of adaptive
quadrature on oscillatory integrands. The corrected apparent rate ($\alpha \approx 0.071$
from $N \ge 1000$) is still decreasing and not yet asymptotic; power-law and logarithmic
models are numerically indistinguishable at this scale, with both extrapolating to
$d_{10^5} \approx 0.058$--$0.061$. This underscores that the Nyman--Beurling
approach cannot be made conclusive by finite computation alone, but the data are
fully consistent with RH.

Our principal analytic contribution is the unconditional proof that $\lambda_n > 0$ for
all $n \le 85$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:pair_pos}), extended to all $n \le 7.1\times10^6$
using the Odlyzko 2M verified-zero dataset, and further to all $n \le 4.83\times10^{12}$
using the Platt--Trudgian verification of the first $3\times10^{12}$ zeros \cite{platt2021}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:multi_zero}).
The key structural insight is that the functional pair $F_n(\sigma,\gamma)$ is strictly
positive for all $\sigma \in (0,1)$ and all $\gamma \ge \gamma_{k+1}$ whenever the
boundary condition $n\arctan(1/\gamma_{k+1}) + \arctan(\sinh(n\log r_{k+1})) < 2\pi$ holds,
by a monotonicity argument on $h(\sigma) = \cosh(n\log r)\cos(n\varphi)$ exploiting
$dr/d\sigma < 0$ and $d\varphi/d\sigma > 0$.  This certifies positivity unconditionally
on the tail zeros without assuming their location.  Corollary~\ref{cor:n86} further
extends the certified range through a quasi-periodic failure-window analysis,
confirming $\lambda_n > 0$ at $13{,}881$ right-endpoint values in 145 certifiable runs
up to $n = 9{,}834{,}937$.  The irreducible gap---verifying $\lambda_n > 0$ for \emph{all}
$n$---remains logically equivalent to RH and cannot be closed by finite computation.

The Lean~4 formalizations provide machine-checked infrastructure for future work.
