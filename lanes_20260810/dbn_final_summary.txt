De Bruijn–Newman kernel definition:
  H_t(z) := ∫₀^∞ e^{tu²} Φ(u) cos(zu) du
  Λ := inf{t : H_t has only real zeros} (RH ⟺ Λ ≤ 0)

  • e^{tu²} in Fourier multiplier sense: modifies the u-spectrum of Φ
  • t > 0: AMPLIFIES high frequencies (|u| >> 1) → sharper oscillations → zeros OFF critical line
  • t < 0: DAMPS high frequencies (|u| >> 1) → smoother function → zeros TOWARD critical line
  ⟹ t < 0 is the "toward real zeros" direction

  • Q_t(g,g) = Q(g_t, g_t) where ĝ_t(u) = e^{(t/2)u²} ĝ(u)

  • At t=0: κ(0) = 1 (one negative square, pinned by Π₁ theory)
  • Direction t<0: should preserve or reduce κ (primes lose power → form becomes PSD-like? No, arch is PSD but pole absorbs)
  • Direction t>0: should increase κ (primes gain power → more negative eigenvalues)
